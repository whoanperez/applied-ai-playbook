# Review ledger — Project ATLAS, AI Assistant for Citizen Services (implementation plan)
Judged by: LCSA Executive Board (approves the plan and the year-1 budget) · Standard: a demanding board expects numbers that add up, a timeline that is achievable, legal and privacy obligations for citizen data and for automated decisions met before approval, an honest account of who is affected (citizens and staff), a full multi-year cost, and measures that prove the stated objective, with failure modes and fallbacks.

## Rubric (fixed — do not change between versions)
| Criterion | 1 = | 3 = | 5 = |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consistency (totals, counts, dates, cross-references) | Several contradictions that change what the Board approves (budget, timeline, scope) | Mostly consistent; a few small slips that do not change the decision | Every total, count, date and cross-reference checks out across all sections and the summary |
| Evidence for claims | Key figures and the main target have no source or derivation | Baselines stated with some source; target partly justified | Every key figure sourced or measured; the target is derived from evidence (pilot, benchmark or calculation) |
| Obligations (privacy, automated decisions, contract terms) | Plan does things a regulator or court would likely object to (sensitive data sent out, automated benefit decisions without review) | Obligations recognised and partly handled; gaps remain in contract or process | Obligations named, designed in (minimisation, human review, appeal, contract protections) and sequenced before launch |
| People affected (citizens and staff) | Affected groups ignored or harmed; impact hidden from the decision-maker | Groups identified; some mitigation; impact disclosed but incomplete | Citizens (including those excluded or vulnerable) and staff have concrete safeguards, fallbacks and honest disclosure |
| Feasibility and full cost | Timeline impossible or budget wrong or missing major cost lines | Timeline plausible with risks; budget complete for year 1 only | Timeline sequenced with dependencies; full multi-year cost with recurring costs, savings and contingency |
| Measures and risk management | Measures do not test the objective; risks are token | Measures partly match the objective; main risks listed with partial mitigations | Measures test the objective and quality/safety/equity; risk register owned, specific, with real mitigations and go/no-go gates |

## Findings
| ID | Severity | Where | Finding | Fixed when… | Could also | Status (v1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-01 | Critical | §5 | ATLAS (an LLM) will "issue the decision automatically" on housing subsidy and disability fee waiver, with approval or denial sent within minutes. No human review, no appeal route, no error handling, and it contradicts the AI notice "may contain errors". These are benefit decisions affecting vulnerable people and they carry 38% of the backlog. | No decision on the housing subsidy or the disability fee waiver (at minimum no denial) is issued without review by a named staff role, and the plan describes how a citizen contests a decision. | Use a deterministic rules engine for eligibility and the LLM only to collect and explain; audit sample of approvals; log every decision; tell citizens when AI was involved. Re-derive the time savings if review is added. | Open |
| F-02 | Critical | §6 | Fine-tuning on 5 years / 2.3M case files exported to the vendor, including national ID numbers, contact details and medical certificates, with no minimisation, anonymisation, legal basis or privacy impact assessment. | §6 no longer sends national IDs, contact details or medical certificates to the vendor in identifiable form, or it names the legal basis and a completed privacy/legal clearance that takes place before the export. | Justify fine-tuning against RAG alone; test for memorisation/leakage; retention and deletion of the export; bias check on historical decisions. | Open |
| F-03 | Critical | §8 | The vendor's standard terms let it use interaction data and the agency's case files "to improve its products and models" and host everything in its global cloud. This contradicts §6 and exposes citizen and medical data. Three-year term on standard terms. | The contract terms (or the plan, as a condition of award) prohibit the vendor from using agency data and interactions to train or improve its own products, and state where the data is hosted and that this is permitted for this data. | SLA, audit rights, breach notification, exit and data-deletion terms, liability caps. | Open |
| F-04 | Critical | §8 vs §9 | Procurement takes about four months, but the rollout has Board approval in month 1, build and fine-tuning in month 2 and nationwide go-live in month 3. The vendor does not exist yet during the months when it is supposed to build. The year-1 target (4 days by end of year 1) depends on this timeline. | The rollout starts build after award, and go-live is no earlier than award plus the build period; the target date for the objective is consistent with the new timeline. | Show the critical path with dependencies (legal review, privacy assessment, integration, training). | Open |
| F-05 | Critical | §9 | Month 4 closes 40% of call-center positions and cuts office hours to three days a week, one month after go-live and two months before the month-6 results report. The cuts are fixed on the calendar, not on evidence that ATLAS works. | The position closures and the hours reduction are conditional on results measured and reported to the Board (go/no-go gate), not scheduled for a fixed month. | Define the thresholds that unlock each step (accuracy, resolution time, satisfaction, escalation rate). | Open |
| F-06 | Critical | §5, §7, §9 | No human fallback for citizens: no escalation to a person from the chat, while call-center capacity and office days are cut. Citizens without a smartphone, internet or digital skills (and those whose case ATLAS cannot solve) are left with no channel. | The plan defines a handoff from ATLAS to a human on every channel and keeps a non-digital way to complete each in-scope procedure, with the staffing for it. | Target response times for human escalation; monitor the share of citizens who cannot complete a procedure digitally. | Open |
| F-07 | Important | §1, §10 | The budget lines add up to 750,000 (420+180+95+25+30), but the total is shown as 780,000 and the Board is asked to approve 780,000. Either a 30,000 line is missing or the total is wrong. | The line items sum to the stated total, and §1 requests the same amount. | Add a contingency line. | Open |
| F-08 | Important | §10 | "Years 2 and 3 are expected to be cost-neutral" with no numbers. The platform license alone is 420,000 a year, there are no recurring costs (usage, WhatsApp messaging, support, model updates, evaluation) and the savings are not quantified. The three-year contract commitment is not costed. | A three-year cost table shows recurring costs and quantified savings, and the cost-neutral claim follows from it (or the claim is removed). | Contingency; cost of staff transition; sensitivity if savings arrive later. | Open |
| F-09 | Important | §3 | The objective is to cut resolution time from 11 to 4 working days, but neither success measure (conversations handled, satisfaction) measures resolution time. The plan could "succeed" without reaching its objective. | Average time to resolve a request is a stated success measure with its baseline (11 days) and how it will be measured. | Break it down by procedure; track first-contact resolution. | Open |
| F-10 | Important | §3, §11 | No quality, safety or equity measures: nothing on accuracy of answers or decisions, errors, complaints, escalations to humans or who cannot use ATLAS. Volume (600,000 conversations) rewards deflection, and the end-of-chat survey only captures those who finish. | At least one measure of answer or decision correctness (for example the error rate found in an audit sample) and one of escalation or abandonment are added to §3 with targets. | Report results by channel and by user group; complaints trend. | Open |
| F-11 | Important | §9 | Go-live "for all citizens, on all channels, nationwide" in one step, with no pilot, no phased scope, no rollback plan, even though the solution includes automated decisions and a new integration. | The plan includes a limited pilot (by scope, region or channel) with go/no-go criteria before the nationwide launch. | Rollback and kill-switch procedure; hypercare period. | Open |
| F-12 | Important | §1, §9 | The summary says ATLAS lets staff "focus on complex cases", while §9 closes 40% of call-center positions and cuts office hours. The Board is not told in the summary that jobs and service hours are affected, and no treatment of affected staff is given. | §1 states the planned position closures and hours reduction, and the plan says what happens to the affected staff. | Consult staff and unions early; redeployment and retraining plan; cost of the transition. | Open |
| F-13 | Important | §7 | The interface is "text-only", national language only, "vendor's standard design", for an agency whose scope includes a fee waiver for people with disabilities. No accessibility requirement or alternative for people who cannot use a text chat. | The plan sets an accessibility requirement for the interface (and an accessible alternative for users who cannot use text chat) and checks it before launch. | Test with disability groups; other languages used by citizens; voice option. | Open |
| F-14 | Important | §7 | The risk register has three generic risks and weak mitigations: an AI notice is not a mitigation for a wrong answer; "vendor's security certifications" is not a mitigation for a cyberattack. Missing: data breach or misuse of sensitive data, wrong automated decisions, exclusion of citizens, vendor dependency, delay from procurement. No owner, likelihood or impact. | The register covers privacy, wrong decisions, exclusion and vendor dependency in addition to the existing risks, and each has a mitigation that acts on the risk and an owner. | Likelihood/impact scoring; trigger points that stop or pause the rollout. | Open |
| F-15 | Important | §3, §5 | The 4-day target is not derived. Most of the saving is said to come from automating the two decisions (§5), which F-01 shows cannot go unreviewed; no pilot or benchmark supports 4 days. | The plan shows how 4 days is reached (a calculation by procedure or pilot evidence) consistent with whatever review is kept on the decisions. | Pilot results; sensitivity range. | Open |
| F-16 | Minor | §2 | Key baselines are unsourced: "70% of all inquiries are repetitive", 11 working days, "one call in five" missed, "citizens have asked", and "the national digital government strategy" is not named. | The key baselines state their source or how they were measured (system report, period). | Name the strategy document and the section that calls for AI. | Open |
| F-17 | Minor | §4 | "The agency's 12 highest-volume procedures" but only 9 are listed. | The number in the text matches the list (or the three missing procedures are added). | Show the volume each procedure represents. | Open |
| F-18 | Minor | §5, §8 | §5 says ATLAS "will run on the CSP selected in section 8", but §8 does not select a cloud provider or vendor; it describes the framework agreement and the vendor's standard terms. | The reference resolves: §8 names the CSP/vendor, or §5 says it will be selected in the procurement. | Name the shortlist. | Open |
