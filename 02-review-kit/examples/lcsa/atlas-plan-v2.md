# Project ATLAS — AI Assistant for Citizen Services

**Implementation plan · version 2.0**
**Prepared by:** Digital Transformation Office, Larkfield Citizen Services Agency (LCSA)
**For:** Executive Board approval, November 2026

> Larkfield and the LCSA are fictional. This document is a training example.

> **Changes since v1.0**
> - No fine-tuning on case files; no case data leaves the agency.
> - Eligibility decisions are now reviewed by a caseworker.
> - Pilot and phased rollout instead of a nationwide launch.
> - Contract terms on data hosting, data use and exit.
> - Success measures, timeline and budget revised.

---

## 1. Summary

ATLAS is an AI assistant that will answer citizens' questions and move their requests forward on the web portal and WhatsApp, 24 hours a day. It will take over the most repetitive work of the call center and the service offices, so that staff can focus on complex cases. We ask the Board to approve the plan and a year-1 budget of USD 730,000.

## 2. Context

The LCSA handles about 1.2 million contacts a year through its call center, 14 service offices, the web portal and email. About 70% of all inquiries are repetitive and could be answered automatically. Today a request takes an average of 11 working days to resolve, and the call center misses about one call in five at peak hours.

Citizens have asked for faster answers, and the national digital government strategy calls on agencies to use AI to improve services.

## 3. Objective and success measures

**Objective:** cut the average time to resolve a request from 11 working days to 4 by the end of year 2, with an interim target of 7 days in the pilot districts at month 11.

**Success measures:**
- Average resolution time for in-scope procedures, from the case management system.
- Accuracy: share of answers judged correct in a monthly staff review of sampled conversations (target: 95%).
- Citizen satisfaction with ATLAS of at least 4 out of 5 stars, measured with a survey at the end of each conversation.

## 4. Scope

ATLAS will cover the agency's 12 highest-volume procedures:

1. Address change in the citizen registry
2. Copy of civil registry certificates
3. Appointment booking for the service offices
4. Status of an ongoing request
5. Housing subsidy application
6. Fee waiver for people with disabilities
7. Business license renewal
8. Complaints and suggestions
9. Payment of fees and fines
10. Resident parking permits
11. Property tax payment plans
12. Pet registration

Out of scope for year 1: tax matters and procedures that require a notary.

## 5. Solution

ATLAS uses a large language model with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): it looks up the relevant passages in the procedure manuals and FAQ before answering. It will run on the cloud provider selected through the procurement in section 8 and connect to the case management system through its existing API, so it can check the status of a request and create new ones.

For the **housing subsidy** and the **fee waiver for people with disabilities**, ATLAS will collect the documents and check them against the eligibility rules. Approvals are issued automatically and reach the citizen by WhatsApp or email within minutes. When the rules point to a denial, a caseworker reviews the case and issues the decision within five working days. These two procedures account for 38% of the current backlog, so this is where most of the time savings come from.

Every answer will include the notice: "This answer was generated by AI and may contain errors."

## 6. Data

ATLAS will **not** be fine-tuned on case files; RAG over the manuals and FAQ is enough. To check a request, ATLAS reads only that record through the API, after the citizen has identified themselves. No case data is exported to the vendor.

Uploaded documents, including medical certificates, go straight into the case management system, and conversation logs are pseudonymized. Before the pilot, the Data Protection Officer will complete a data protection impact assessment and confirm the legal basis for the processing.

## 7. Governance and risk

The project team will keep a risk register and review it monthly. The main risks identified so far:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The model gives a wrong answer | AI notice (section 5); monthly accuracy review (section 3) |
| Misuse of personal data | No data export (section 6); contract clauses (section 8) |
| Cyberattack on the platform | Vendor's security certifications; security test before the pilot |
| Staff resistance to change | Communication plan and training |

A steering committee, chaired by the project director with the vendor's delivery lead and the head of the call center, will review the vendor's pilot evaluation and take the go/no-go decision on the rollout.

ATLAS will launch in the national language, as a text-only chat. The interface follows the vendor's standard design.

## 8. Procurement

ATLAS will be procured under the national framework agreement for cloud and AI services. The procurement process (approvals, call for offers, evaluation and award) takes about **four months**.

Instead of the vendor's standard terms, the contract must provide for: hosting in data centers in the country; agency ownership of all data; no use of agency data or interactions to train or improve the vendor's products or models; and, at exit, return of all data and certified deletion within 30 days. The contract term is three years.

## 9. Rollout

| Month | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Board approval, then procurement |
| 5–7 | Build, integration, security testing and impact assessment |
| 8–10 | **Pilot in two districts**, web portal and WhatsApp |
| 11 | Pilot evaluation and go/no-go (section 7) |
| 12 onward | Phased rollout, region by region |

The call center and the 14 offices keep their current hours throughout.

## 10. Budget (year 1, USD)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Platform license | 420,000 |
| Integration with the case management system | 180,000 |
| Impact assessment and security testing | 40,000 |
| Pilot operations and evaluation | 35,000 |
| Staff training | 25,000 |
| Communication campaign | 30,000 |
| **Total** | **730,000** |

The program runs for three years. From year 2, ATLAS is expected to pay for itself, as efficiency gains in the call center and the offices cover the platform.

## 11. Evaluation

At month 6 the Digital Transformation Office will report to the Board on the success measures in section 3 and propose the procedures for phase 2.
