# Meridian Fund for Digital Public Services

> The Meridian Fund, Larkfield and the LCSA are fictional. This call is a training example with 42 requirements.

## Call for Proposals MF-CFP-2026/02 — Responsible Artificial Intelligence in Citizen Services

**Date of publication:** 1 October 2026
**Submission deadline:** 15 January 2027, 17:00 UTC
**Total envelope:** USD 4,200,000

---

## 1. Background and purpose

The Meridian Fund for Digital Public Services ("the Fund") is a multi-donor international cooperation fund that helps public institutions make their services simpler, more accessible and more trustworthy for the people who use them.

This call focuses on the responsible use of artificial intelligence (AI) in citizen-facing services. Public agencies face growing pressure to answer more enquiries, reduce waiting times and offer services outside office hours, often without additional staff. AI assistants can help, but they also raise legitimate concerns about accuracy, privacy, accountability and the exclusion of people who are less familiar with digital tools. The Fund's aim is to finance carefully designed pilots that generate evidence on what works, under conditions that protect the rights of users, so that participating institutions and their peers can take informed decisions about scaling.

The Fund expects to finance between 10 and 14 projects under this call. Proposals will be assessed competitively against the criteria set out in Section 15.

## 2. Eligibility of applicants and projects

Eligible applicants are public-sector entities at national or subnational level, such as ministries, decentralised agencies, municipalities and regional governments, that deliver services directly to the public or are responsible for their delivery.[^1] Private companies, civil society organisations and universities are not eligible as applicants; they may take part in projects as service providers selected under the rules in Section 12, or as partners supporting user engagement.

Eligible projects introduce or improve AI-enabled tools in services that citizens use directly: information and orientation, appointment management, request tracking, issuing of documents, and support to the processing of applications. Because the purpose of the call is to generate evidence before large-scale adoption, projects that deploy AI in citizen services must be designed as pilots limited in scope, for example to a defined number of service points, services or user groups, and must define a go/no-go decision point, with explicit and measurable criteria, at which the applicant will decide whether to scale, adjust or discontinue the solution.

## 3. Duration and timing

The implementation period of each project must be no shorter than 12 months and no longer than 24 months. Grant agreements under this call are expected to be signed during March 2027; accordingly, the earliest permitted start date of the implementation period is 1 April 2027, and no project activity may begin, and no cost may be incurred, before that date. All projects must end no later than 31 December 2028, whatever their duration. Requests for no-cost extensions are handled during implementation, in exceptional circumstances only, and play no part in the selection process.

## 4. Funding, co-financing and indirect costs

- The grant requested from the Fund must be no less than USD 150,000 and no more than USD 400,000.
- Applicants must provide co-financing equal to at least 20% of the total project cost (as defined in Annex I). Co-financing may be provided in cash or in kind.
- In-kind contributions (for example, staff time, use of premises or of existing equipment) must be valued at cost, and the valuation method must be explained in the budget narrative.
- Indirect costs may be charged as a flat rate of up to 7% of total direct eligible costs. Indirect costs do not need to be supported by accounting documents.

The grant will be paid in three instalments: a first pre-financing payment on signature of the grant agreement, a second payment on approval of the second semi-annual progress report, and the balance on approval of the final report.

## 5. Eligible and ineligible costs

Costs are eligible when they are necessary for the project, incurred during the implementation period, identifiable in the applicant's accounts and included in the approved budget. Eligible direct costs include personnel, contracted services, equipment, software and cloud services, training, user research, communication, and monitoring and evaluation. Staff recruited specifically for the project on fixed-term contracts are eligible for grant funding, whereas the salaries of the applicant's permanent staff are not eligible for grant funding, although the time that permanent staff devote to the project may be counted as in-kind co-financing. The Fund recognises that pilots often need a modest amount of hardware at service points, such as kiosks or tablets; equipment of all kinds may nevertheless not exceed 10% of total direct costs. In the same spirit, software licences, subscriptions and cloud services are eligible only for the portion of their term that falls within the implementation period: where a provider requires or offers payment for a longer term, the share corresponding to any period after the end date of the project is not eligible and must be covered from other sources, even when it is paid during the implementation period.

The following costs are not eligible under any circumstances:

- purchase of land or buildings, and construction works;
- purchase of vehicles;
- debts, fines, interest charges and exchange-rate losses;
- costs already financed by another donor or by another grant of the Fund;
- contingency reserves or unallocated budget lines.

## 6. Structure, format and language of proposals

Proposals must be written in English or Spanish. The narrative (Sections 1 to 10 below) must not exceed 15 A4 pages, in a font size of at least 11 points; annexes do not count towards this limit.

The narrative must contain the following ten sections, in this order:

1. **Summary** — no more than 300 words, stating the problem, the proposed solution, the main expected results, the requested grant and the total project cost.
2. **Context and problem** — the services concerned, their users and evidence of the problem the project addresses.
3. **Objectives and theory of change** — the general and specific objectives and the logic linking activities to outcomes.
4. **Activities and work plan** — the activities; a timeline by month or by quarter; management arrangements and key personnel; the procurement plan; and the reporting and evaluation arrangements.
5. **Results framework** — outcome and output indicators (see Section 7).
6. **Inclusion and accessibility** — see Section 8.
7. **Data protection and ethics of AI** — see Section 9.
8. **Risk management** — see Section 10.
9. **Sustainability** — see Section 11.
10. **Budget and budget narrative** — a summary budget table by cost line, with separate columns for the grant and for co-financing, and a narrative justifying each line.

The following annexes must accompany the proposal:

- the logical framework (logframe) of the project;
- the detailed budget, prepared in the Fund's spreadsheet template;
- a letter committing the co-financing, signed by the applicant's legal representative;
- the data protection impact assessment (DPIA) of the project (see Section 9);
- the curricula vitae of the key personnel (see Annex I);
- at least one letter of support from an organisation representing the users of the services concerned (for example, an association of persons with disabilities, of older persons or of consumers).

## 7. Results framework

The results framework presented in Section 5 of the proposal, and reproduced in the logframe, distinguishes outcomes, meaning the changes in service delivery and in the experience of users that the project expects to bring about, from outputs, meaning the products and services that the project itself delivers. The Fund encourages applicants to keep the framework short, typically with three to six outcome indicators, and to include at least one indicator on user experience. Every outcome indicator must have a quantified baseline value, stating the year to which the value refers and its source.[^2] Every indicator, whether at outcome or at output level, must have a quantified target for the end of the project and a means of verification.

## 8. Gender, inclusion and accessibility

AI-enabled services can narrow gaps in access to public services, but they can also widen them, for example when they assume a level of digital skill, literacy or connectivity that not all users have, or when they are tested only with the users who are easiest to reach. For this reason, Section 6 of the proposal must include a gender and inclusion analysis that identifies the groups at risk of exclusion from the services concerned (for example women, older persons, persons with disabilities, people with low literacy or with limited connectivity) and the measures the project will take to address the risks identified. All digital interfaces developed or modified with the grant, including conversational interfaces, web pages and self-service kiosks, must conform to the applicable national web accessibility standard at its intermediate conformance level or higher. The introduction of an AI-enabled channel must never become a barrier: no service covered by the project may become available exclusively through that channel, and the existing in-person and telephone channels must remain available on equal terms throughout the project. Finally, the testing of the solution with users, before and during the pilot, must include persons with disabilities and older persons (aged 65 or over).

## 9. Data protection and ethics of AI

Compliance with the applicable data protection law of the applicant's jurisdiction is a condition of every grant agreement. At proposal stage, Section 7 of the proposal must describe the legal basis for each processing of personal data, the categories of personal data processed and the retention periods applied, and the DPIA listed among the annexes must cover the processing carried out in the pilot. In addition, the following conditions apply to all projects under this call:

- Personal data processed in the project may not be used to train, fine-tune or otherwise improve AI models, whether by the applicant or by its providers, and contracts with providers must exclude such use.
- Decisions that grant, deny, modify or revoke benefits, subsidies, exemptions or other entitlements must be taken by a human officer. AI may support the preparation of such decisions, for example by checking that documents are complete, but may not take them. Purely informational or transactional operations that involve no discretion, such as booking appointments, reporting the status of a request or issuing certificates that reproduce existing records, may be automated.
- Users must be clearly informed that they are interacting with an AI system and must be able to reach a human agent at any point of the interaction.

## 10. Risk management

Section 8 of the proposal must present a risk matrix. The Fund expects applicants to consider, among others, technological risks, risks to personal data, risks relating to the performance of the AI system, risks of low adoption by staff or users, and institutional risks. The risk matrix must indicate, for each risk identified, its likelihood, its impact, the mitigation measures and the risk owner, meaning the person or unit responsible for monitoring the risk and applying the mitigation measures.

## 11. Sustainability

Section 9 of the proposal must explain how the solution will be operated after the grant ends. Applicants must commit to funding the operating costs of the solution for at least 12 months after the end of the implementation period, and must identify the source of those funds, such as a specific line of the institutional budget. The Fund will also value credible plans for scaling the solution if the pilot is successful.

## 12. Procurement

Grantees apply their own procurement rules, provided that those rules ensure transparency, equal treatment and value for money. In addition, any contract financed by the grant with an estimated value above USD 50,000 must be awarded through a competitive procedure in which at least three valid offers are obtained; where fewer than three valid offers are received, the procedure must be relaunched or the prior approval of the Fund obtained.

## 13. Visibility

Visibility costs must appear as a separate line of the budget and may not exceed 2% of the grant requested. The support of the Fund must be acknowledged, using its logo, in all communication materials, in publications and in the user interfaces of the solution.

## 14. Reporting, monitoring and evaluation

Grantees must submit a narrative and financial progress report for every six-month period of implementation, within 30 days of the end of that period, and a final narrative and financial report within 60 days of the end date of the project. Each project must include an independent external final evaluation.[^3]

## 15. Submission and selection

Proposals must be submitted through the Fund's online portal no later than 15 January 2027 at 17:00 UTC, with the narrative as a single PDF file, the detailed budget uploaded in its original spreadsheet format, and the other annexes as separate PDF files. Proposals received by other means or after the deadline will not be considered.

Questions may be sent to the Fund's helpdesk until 15 December 2026; answers will be published on the portal for all applicants. Eligible proposals will be scored by an independent panel against the following criteria: relevance and evidence of the problem (25 points); quality of design and methodology (25 points); protection of rights and inclusion (20 points); sustainability and potential for scaling (15 points); and value for money (15 points). Results will be communicated to all applicants by the end of February 2027.

## Annex I — Definitions

- **Direct eligible costs:** costs directly attributable to project activities that meet the conditions of Section 5, whether financed by the grant or by co-financing.
- **Total project cost:** the sum of total direct eligible costs and indirect costs, including both the grant and co-financing.
- **Key personnel:** at a minimum, the project lead and the technical lead, the latter being the person responsible for the AI solution.
- **Legal representative:** the person who, under the founding instrument of the applicant, is authorised to commit the entity legally.
- **Implementation period:** the period between the start date and the end date of the project set in the grant agreement.

---

[^1]: Applicants must have been legally constituted for at least three years on the date of the submission deadline. Entities created by merger or restructuring may count the legal existence of a predecessor entity that delivered the same services.

[^2]: Output indicators that measure new products or services may have a baseline of zero. Where the applicant plans a survey during the project to refine existing data, the value available at the time of submission should still be reported as the baseline.

[^3]: For the purposes of this call, an evaluation is independent when the evaluator has had no contractual or hierarchical relationship with the applicant for the delivery of project activities. The final evaluation must be budgeted at no less than 3% of the grant requested. The Fund will review the terms of reference of the evaluation before the evaluator is contracted.
