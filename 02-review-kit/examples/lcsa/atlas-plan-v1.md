# Project ATLAS — AI Assistant for Citizen Services

**Implementation plan · version 1.0**
**Prepared by:** Digital Transformation Office, Larkfield Citizen Services Agency (LCSA)
**For:** Executive Board approval, November 2026

> Larkfield and the LCSA are fictional. This document is a training example.

---

## 1. Summary

ATLAS is an AI assistant that will answer citizens' questions and move their requests forward on the web portal and WhatsApp, 24 hours a day. It will take over the most repetitive work of the call center and the service offices, so that staff can focus on complex cases. We ask the Board to approve the plan and a year-1 budget of USD 780,000.

## 2. Context

The LCSA handles about 1.2 million contacts a year through its call center, 14 service offices, the web portal and email. 70% of all inquiries are repetitive and could be answered automatically. Today a request takes an average of 11 working days to resolve, and the call center misses about one call in five at peak hours.

Citizens have asked for faster answers, and the national digital government strategy calls on agencies to use AI to improve services.

## 3. Objective and success measures

**Objective:** cut the average time to resolve a request from 11 working days to 4 by the end of year 1.

**Success measures:**
- Number of conversations handled by ATLAS (target: 600,000 in year 1).
- Citizen satisfaction with ATLAS of at least 4 out of 5 stars, measured with a survey at the end of each conversation.

## 4. Scope

ATLAS will cover the agency's 12 highest-volume procedures:

1. Address change in the citizen registry
2. Copy of civil registry certificates
3. Appointment booking for the service offices
4. Status of an ongoing request
5. Housing subsidy application
6. Fee waiver for people with disabilities
7. Business license renewal
8. Complaints and suggestions
9. Payment of fees and fines

Out of scope for year 1: tax matters and procedures that require a notary.

## 5. Solution

ATLAS uses a large language model with RAG over the agency's procedure manuals and FAQ. It will run on the CSP selected in section 8 and connect to the case management system through its existing API, so it can check the status of a request and create new ones.

For the **housing subsidy** and the **fee waiver for people with disabilities**, ATLAS will collect the documents, check them against the eligibility rules and **issue the decision automatically**. The citizen receives the approval or denial by WhatsApp or email within minutes. These two procedures account for 38% of the current backlog, so automating the decision is where most of the time savings come from.

Every answer will include the notice: "This answer was generated by AI and may contain errors."

## 6. Data

To make ATLAS accurate on our procedures, we will **fine-tune the model on five years of case files** (about 2.3 million records) exported from the case management system. The export includes the full case history, citizens' national ID numbers, contact details and supporting documents, including the medical certificates submitted for disability fee waivers. Using real cases is the best way to teach the model how our staff actually decide.

The export will be sent to the vendor in month 2 over a secure file transfer.

## 7. Governance and risk

The project team will keep a risk register and review it monthly. The main risks identified so far:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The model gives a wrong answer | AI notice on every answer (section 5) |
| Cyberattack on the platform | Vendor's security certifications |
| Staff resistance to change | Communication plan and training |

ATLAS will launch in the national language, as a text-only chat. The interface follows the vendor's standard design.

## 8. Procurement

ATLAS will be procured under the national framework agreement for cloud and AI services. The procurement process (approvals, call for offers, evaluation and award) takes about **four months**.

The proposed contract uses the vendor's standard terms. Among them: the solution is hosted in the vendor's global cloud, and the vendor may use interaction data and the case files provided by the agency to improve its products and models. The contract term is three years.

## 9. Rollout

| Month | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 | Board approval |
| 2 | Build, integration and fine-tuning |
| 3 | **Go-live for all citizens, on all channels, nationwide** |
| 4 | Close 40% of call-center positions and reduce office opening hours to three days a week |
| 6 | Results report to the Board |

## 10. Budget (year 1, USD)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Platform license | 420,000 |
| Integration with the case management system | 180,000 |
| Fine-tuning | 95,000 |
| Staff training | 25,000 |
| Communication campaign | 30,000 |
| **Total** | **780,000** |

The program runs for three years. Years 2 and 3 are expected to be cost-neutral, because savings from the call center and the offices will cover the platform.

## 11. Evaluation

At month 6 the Digital Transformation Office will report to the Board on the success measures in section 3 and propose the procedures for phase 2.
