## 1. Summary

The Larkfield Citizen Services Agency (LCSA) handles about 410,000 office visits a year. More than half concern simple matters (information, appointments, request status, standard certificates), yet they cause average waits of 38 minutes at our busiest offices, while many applications for the housing subsidy and the disability fee waiver are returned because documents are missing.

We propose a 12-month pilot of ATLAS, an AI assistant for citizen services, in two service offices (Northgate and Riverside) and on the LCSA web portal. ATLAS will answer questions using only LCSA's service manuals (retrieval, with no training on personal data) and will complete low-risk procedures: appointments, request status and certificates. For the housing subsidy and the disability fee waiver, it will only pre-check documents; a case officer takes every decision. In-person and telephone channels remain unchanged.

We expect to cut average waits at the pilot offices from 38 to 22 minutes, double the share of simple procedures completed without a visit, and raise user satisfaction from 3.4 to 4.0 out of 5. A go/no-go decision in month 17 will determine whether ATLAS is extended to LCSA's other nine offices.

The project lasts 18 months and is led by LCSA's Digital Transformation Office. We request a grant of USD 365,200; the total project cost is USD 453,200.

## 2. Context and problem

LCSA was created by regional ordinance in 2015 as the single front office of the Larkfield regional government. It operates 11 service offices, a call centre and a web portal.

LCSA's 2026 service records show three connected problems. First, 58% of office visits relate to information or to low-risk procedures that do not require a case officer's judgement. At the two busiest offices, Northgate and Riverside, the queue management system records an average wait of 38 minutes. Second, only 22% of low-risk requests from the two offices' catchment areas are completed without a visit, mainly because portal information is hard to find. Third, 31% of applications for the housing subsidy and the disability fee waiver are returned as incomplete. Because the current case management system registers each resubmission as a new file, LCSA cannot reliably measure the time from first submission to decision for these applications.

According to the 2026 user survey (n = 1,180), 34% of office visitors are aged 60 or over, 12% report a disability, 57% are women and 18% have no home internet access.

## 3. Objectives and theory of change

**General objective:** to make LCSA's citizen services faster, easier and more inclusive through the responsible use of AI.

**Specific objectives:**

- SO1. Resolve information requests and low-risk procedures through ATLAS, reducing avoidable visits and waiting times.
- SO2. Reduce incomplete housing subsidy and disability fee waiver applications through document pre-checks, with decisions kept by case officers.
- SO3. Generate evidence on accuracy, user experience and inclusion for an informed decision on scaling.

**Theory of change.** If residents can get reliable answers and complete simple procedures at any time through an assistant that draws only on LCSA's approved manuals, and if applicants learn before submitting which documents are missing, then fewer people will queue and fewer files will be returned, freeing case officers for complex cases. This assumes that ATLAS answers accurately and that people who prefer human service keep receiving it on equal terms; quality sampling, accessibility testing and unchanged human channels address these assumptions.

## 6. Inclusion and accessibility

**Gender and inclusion analysis.** The 2026 user survey and staff focus groups identify five groups at risk of exclusion: older persons, who are a third of visitors and use the portal less; persons with visual, hearing or cognitive disabilities; women with caring responsibilities, who are 64% of housing subsidy applicants and find office hours difficult; people with low literacy; and residents without home internet. The main risks are an assistant these groups find hard to use and the neglect of the human channels they rely on.

**Measures.** ATLAS will use plain language and a read-aloud option; kiosks offer voice interaction and adjustable text size. The conversational interface, the portal pages and the kiosk and tablet interfaces will conform to the national web accessibility standard at its intermediate conformance level, verified by an external audit before go-live and again in month 12. Usability testing before go-live will involve 60 users, including at least 15 persons with disabilities and 15 persons aged 65 or over, recruited with the Larkfield Disability Rights Network and the Larkfield Senior Citizens Council; the two rounds of testing during the pilot will follow the same quotas. At least half of the participants will be women, with sessions at varied times to suit caregivers. All pilot services will remain available in person and by telephone, with unchanged opening hours and staffing, during and after the project.

## 7. Data protection and ethics of AI

**Legal basis.** All processing is based on the performance of LCSA's public-service tasks under its founding ordinance; for health-related data in disability certificates, also on the provision of the applicable data protection law for the management of social benefits.

**Categories of data.** Identification and contact data; request and appointment references and status; documents uploaded for pre-check, which may include income statements and disability certificates; and conversation logs.

**Retention.** Conversation logs are pseudonymised at collection, kept for 90 days for quality sampling and then deleted. Uploaded documents, pre-check results and request references are stored in the case file and follow its retention period under LCSA's records schedule (five years). Appointment data are kept for two years, as for existing channels.

**DPIA.** A DPIA covering all processing in the pilot was completed in November 2026 and reviewed by LCSA's Data Protection Officer (Annex D).

**No training on personal data.** ATLAS generates answers only from LCSA's approved manuals through retrieval; no model is trained or fine-tuned. The contracts with the platform and integration providers will exclude any use of LCSA data to train or improve their models.

**Human decisions.** ATLAS checks whether applications for the housing subsidy and the disability fee waiver are complete and tells applicants which documents are missing; it cannot approve, reject or modify an application. Every decision is taken by a case officer. Certificates issued by ATLAS only reproduce existing records.

**Transparency and human contact.** Every conversation, on the portal and at the kiosks, opens with a clear notice that the user is talking to an AI assistant. A "talk to a person" option is available at every step and transfers the user to a staff member by chat or, outside chat hours, to the call centre's 24-hour telephone line.

## 8. Risk management

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ATLAS gives incorrect or invented answers | Medium | High | Answers restricted to approved manuals with source shown; monthly quality sampling; automatic handover when confidence is low |
| Personal data breach | Low | High | DPIA measures; pseudonymised logs; security audit and penetration test before go-live |
| Low adoption by staff | Medium | Medium | Early involvement of front-line staff in design; training; feedback channel |
| Low uptake or exclusion of older users and persons with disabilities | Medium | High | Accessibility audit; testing quotas; assisted kiosks; unchanged human channels |
| Delay in the integration tender | Medium | Medium | Tender documents prepared in advance; integration and testing activities overlap in the work plan |
| Change in institutional priorities after the regional budget cycle | Low | High | Director-General commitment letter; operating costs included in the draft institutional budget |

The risk matrix will be reviewed quarterly by the steering committee and updated in each progress report.

## 9. Sustainability

The operating cost of ATLAS at the scale of the pilot is estimated at USD 52,000 a year (platform subscription, maintenance and one content specialist). LCSA commits to funding the operation of ATLAS at the two pilot offices and on the portal for at least 24 months after 31 August 2028, from its regular operating budget (programme 03, "Digital channels"), as confirmed in the Director-General's letter (Annex C). The go/no-go decision concerns extension to the other nine offices, which would be financed from the 2029 regional budget.
