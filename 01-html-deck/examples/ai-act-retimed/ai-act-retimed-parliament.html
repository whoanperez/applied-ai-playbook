What happened

Nothing was repealed. The clock moved.

The Digital Omnibus on AI amends the AI Act. It entered into force on 27 July 2026, days before the original high-risk deadline.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, EUR-Lex.

The new calendar

High-risk systems gained more than a year

  • Annex III 2 Aug 2026 → 2 Dec 2027 (hiring, credit scoring, public services)
  • Annex I 2 Aug 2027 → 2 Aug 2028 (AI inside regulated products)

Halden Observatory07

What changed, what didn't

Four smaller changes reach beyond the deadlines

Marking

Generative systems already on the market have until 2 Dec 2026 to mark AI-generated content.

New prohibitions

Article 5 now bans non-consensual intimate images of real people and child sexual abuse material.

AI literacy

Article 4 now asks organisations to support staff AI literacy, not to ensure a set level.

Small mid-caps

A new category of company gets simplifications that were reserved for SMEs.

Sources: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744; Gibson Dunn and Orrick report the new bans apply from 2 Dec 2026.

Halden Observatory08

What changed, what didn't

The Omnibus is not a pause

Three sets of duties already apply. The risk-based structure of the AI Act stays; what moved is when parts of it apply.

  • Prohibited practices, since 2 Feb 2025
  • General-purpose AI models, since 2 Aug 2025
  • Transparency duties, since 2 Aug 2026

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 113.
